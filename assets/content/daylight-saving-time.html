If your timings look one hour ahead or behind after a daylight saving time (DST) change, check both the masjid account and the TV player. The portal holds your masjid's time zone and prayer timetable; the player also has its own clock and time-zone settings.

Start by identifying what is wrong.

Compare the current clock on the TV, the timings in the Masjidal portal, and your masjid's approved timetable. Correcting a device clock does not edit your saved Salah or Iqamah entries.

1. Check your masjid account's time zone

  1. Sign in to the Masjidal portal.
  2. Open Account → Masjid Info. This is the masjid account-information page.
  3. Check the masjid's address and location. Find the Timezone field and select the time zone appropriate to your masjid's location.
  4. Choose the correct regional/city time zone rather than switching regions to compensate for a one-hour difference. For example, a masjid in the Chicago region should use its appropriate Chicago time zone, not change to an Eastern region to make the clock look right.
  5. Click Save if you made a correction.
  6. Open Timings → Salah Timings and Timings → Iqamah Timings. Check the date and compare the displayed times with the timetable approved by your masjid.

If the account's time zone is correct but the timetable is still wrong, review how your timings are maintained. Calculated Salah times and an uploaded timetable require different checks. Follow Salah (Athan) for those settings.

Review before regenerating timings.

Saving changes to Salah calculation methods or offsets regenerates timings and overrides existing entries. Do not apply a blanket one-hour offset just to compensate for an incorrect account or device time zone.

Check Iqamah change dates separately

Iqamah is the congregation time chosen by your masjid. Check whether a new Iqamah entry is needed for the date of the DST change. A saved entry remains in effect until the next change date. Follow Iqamah to review, add, or edit those entries.

2. Check the TV player's own clock

Use this section if the TV's current clock is wrong, especially when the portal or another TV shows the correct time. These steps apply to the Al-Iqamah TV/signage player, rather than the separate Athan Frame setup.

Open the device settings

  1. On the TV's prayer-time screen, click the Masjidal logo in the bottom-right corner. A USB mouse connected to the player can make this easier.
  2. On the Al-Iqamah settings screen, select Android Settings.
  3. Find Date & time. Depending on the player's Android version, it may appear directly in settings or under System. Menu names and placement vary by device.

Check the date, time, and time zone

  1. Make sure the player is connected to the internet.
  2. If available, enable Automatic date & time or the equivalent network-provided time option.
  3. Check the selected Time zone. If the device supports automatic time-zone detection, verify that it selects the correct region. Otherwise, choose the appropriate regional time zone manually.
  4. Check the date and the current local time. Avoid manually adding or subtracting one hour while leaving an incorrect time zone selected.
  5. Return to Al-Iqamah and compare the current clock and prayer timetable again. Repeat the device checks on any other affected players.

If the player clock is still out of sync after checking its time zone and internet connection, follow Current Time Sync (Signage Player). That guide covers the NTP synchronization app for the signage player.

3. Confirm that the issue is resolved

  • The TV's current clock matches the correct local time.
  • The masjid account has the correct regional time zone.
  • Salah and Iqamah times for the current date match your masjid's approved timetable.
  • Other TVs using the same account also show the expected clock and timings.

Still one hour off?

Contact masjidsupport@masjidal.com with your masjid name and location, player/device type, the selected account and device time zones, a photo of the clock and timings, and the expected times. Mention whether the portal, all TVs, or only one TV is affected.
